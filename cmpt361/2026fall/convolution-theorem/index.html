The Convolution Theorem

Sliding a kernel over every pixel is a lot of arithmetic. Transform both sides, multiply them pixel by pixel, transform back, and the answer is identical to machine precision. This figure runs both routes on the same data and prints the residual.

Both routes wrap around

The FFT treats the image as tiled infinitely, so a kernel hanging off the right edge reads pixels from the left. The direct sum here wraps the same way, which is what makes the residual pure rounding noise. Zero-pad both signals to N + K − 1 when you want the non-circular answer.

Zero frequency sits at the center

Spectra are drawn fftshifted. The middle pixel is u = v = 0, the image mean. Distance from the middle is spatial frequency in cycles per image, and direction is the orientation of that wave.

|G| is the filter, read directly

Pick the box blur and look at |G|: a bright center fading outward, which attenuates the high frequencies carrying the edges. Pick the Laplacian of Gaussian and |G| turns into a ring, dark at the center, which is a band-pass filter drawn as a picture.

The kernel really is 128 × 128

Multiplying spectra needs both arrays the same size, so g is zero-padded to the full image with its center at pixel (0, 0), split across the four corners. The g panel shows a zoomed, recentered crop of it.
