The camera always aims at the tetrahedron. Roll spins it about its own optical axis — c does not move, yet R changes completely.

What to watch. The four world coordinates are integers and they never move — the tetrahedron is nailed to the world. Everything that changes when you jump the camera lives in those twelve numbers. Watch the rows of R: they are literally the camera's right, up and backward axes, which is why they are drawn in the same three colours on the frustum. Watch t: it is not the camera position, it is −Rc, the world origin expressed in camera coordinates — which is why it swings wildly even when you only move c a little. Then press Play. Step ① slides the whole scene by t = −c until the camera sits on the origin; step ② rotates it by R until the camera's own axes are the axes. Nothing physical moves — the ground grid is still the same grid, it just arrives tilted, because we are now writing the world down from the camera's point of view. The coordinates on the vertices change as it goes, and land on exactly the numbers in the table. Note the two steps carry different translations: the 4×4 on the right holds −c, but multiplying the pair out gives [R | −Rc], which is the 3×4 above. And watch the roll slider, which leaves c untouched while rewriting all nine entries of R: position and orientation really are independent, and the matrix holds both.
