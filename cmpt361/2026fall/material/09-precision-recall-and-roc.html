Feature matching: what counts as a true positive?

Eight features in image A, eight in image B, so 64 candidate pairs — and the matcher gives every one of them a score. You pick a threshold: accept the pair as a match, or reject it. Features are numbered so that Ai truly corresponds to Bi (B?1 and B?2 are spurious detections with no partner), so six of the 64 pairs are the real correspondences and the other 58 are not.

Every pair is a sample here, which is what makes FPR, specificity and ROC well defined: the negatives are the 58 pairs that do not correspond, and a true negative is one of those the threshold rejected. Nearly all of them are trivially easy (A1 against a dot on the far side of the image), which is why the TN count is huge, why accuracy is useless, and why the matching literature usually plots precision–recall instead. Picking one partner per feature (a one-to-one assignment, mutual nearest neighbour, ratio test) is a separate step that happens after this scoring — it prunes the 64 pairs down before any threshold is applied. ROC: each threshold gives one (FPR, TPR) dot; sweeping it from 1 down to 0 traces the curve, whose area (AUC) is the probability that a true correspondence outscores a wrong pair. The good matcher bulges toward the top-left corner; the weak one hugs the diagonal, and no threshold rescues it.
