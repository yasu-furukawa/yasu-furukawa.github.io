Eigenvectors as Principal Axes

A symmetric matrix A maps the unit circle to an ellipse. Almost every vector comes out pointing somewhere new — but two perpendicular directions come out pointing the same way, only rescaled. Those are the eigenvectors, they are exactly the ellipse's principal axes, and turning the coordinate frame onto them makes A diagonal with the eigenvalues on the diagonal.

What to look for

Three observations, in the order they build on each other. Each button sets the figure up for you.

01

Drag v around the circle and watch Av. It generally swings away from v. Exactly twice per half-turn it lines up — and only then is Av a plain multiple of v.

02

The circle becomes an ellipse. Its long and short axes point along those two directions, and their half-lengths are |λ₁| and |λ₂| — the stretch factors along each.

03

Now turn the frame with φ. When u₁, u₂ sit on the principal axes, the off-diagonals vanish and the diagonal reads λ₁, λ₂. Same matrix — better axes.
