The four numbers you tuned

Your curve is built from two shifted cosines, nothing more:

y(t) = A₁cos(2πk₁t − φ₁) + A₂cos(2πk₂t − φ₂)

Amplitude sets how tall each wave is; shift sets where in its cycle it starts. Two numbers per frequency, and no fewer will do — a single cosine can only ever start at its peak.

Drag the sliders and watch the dots below. Amplitude pushes a dot away from the origin; shift swings it around. The two knobs move the dot in polar coordinates — which is the whole reason the pair wants to live in a plane.

What the transform hands back

Feed the curve through the DFT, scaled so the numbers come out at curve-sized values:

ck = (2/N) Σn y[n] e−2πikn/N

Almost every ck is zero. At k₁ and k₂ you get a non-zero complex number — the dots below — and each carries exactly the two things you tuned:

|ck| = A    arg ck = −φ

The length is the amplitude and the angle is the shift. The minus sign is bookkeeping from the e−iθ in the definition — write the transform with e+iθ, as some textbooks do, and the angle is φ outright. Either way it is one number holding two, which is what complex numbers are for.

You can drag the dots directly. Moving one is the same as moving that wave's two sliders, because they are the same two numbers in different clothes.
